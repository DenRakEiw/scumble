What the numbers say:
- One light (and a sun) is below q6.7's 0.85; three lights are at or above it. Two parts lose it. The idle share
  (1 - busy: 2-11 % for the twin's lights, 19-22 % for the sun, whose stripes take only 60-75 ms): the tail of 32
  stripes on 8 workers and their dispatch. And the stripes' kernels running slower than the same work as one job alone
  (x 1.07-1.39): eight busy cores against one (all-core clocks, the memory and caches of the two CCDs shared).
  Shared-memory wasm threads remove neither: the twin, which marches every point and spot light, is JS and reads the
  shared depth map in place (nothing is copied for it), and the Rust sun copies the map once a worker (_slot). What
  threads could win is the per-stripe dispatch, a part of the idle share.
- Why the named one-light row's stripes run slower than the re-march (kernel x 1.39 against x 1.14; after_build 1.39
  against 1.09; the review's question): not other pool work and not mips (`others`: no other pool job and no mip chain
  built or asked in any named, re-march or one-job row of any run, but r3's three-light one-job row, beside which 249
  other jobs ran), and not a warm-up (each worker's first stripe runs as fast as the re-march's, 114-119 against 112-117
  ms in r1 and r2, its later ones slower, 164-172 against 124-125; r3 is slower throughout). It is the window's work after the release. ../diag/ moves light
  0 and marches it with the window left alone (no markFilterChanged, renderLayers or draw until it has landed), then
  moves it and releases it as the named row does, each against its own one-job march in the same run. Released is below
  left alone in all three runs (0.604 / 0.648, 0.682 / 0.874, 0.595 / 0.659); in r2, the run the other agent's job
  loaded least, left alone is as efficient as the re-march (0.874, kernel x 1.10) and released is the named row (0.682,
  x 1.35). So what slows the named row is the window and the GPU process drawing the moved light's shading beside the
  workers (inferred: the same cores and memory), not the march's scaling, and wasm threads would not remove that
  either. ../diag/ ran beside the other agent's torch job (the GPU at 44-53 % and 9 GB throughout), so only its pairs
  within a run are used, not its walls.
- Stop 5 decides: by q6.7's letter the one-light efficiency (0.67 named, 0.73 the march alone) is below 0.85; by its
  reason (the spike can win at most the stripe overhead, §4.5's "about 2 %") the loss lies elsewhere. Not measured: more
  stripes a worker (STRIPES_PER_WORKER 8: a shorter tail, more dispatch).
